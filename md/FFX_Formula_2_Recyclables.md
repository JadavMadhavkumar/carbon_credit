**Recyclables Carbon Credit Formulas**

Recyclable materials — plastics, paper, glass, and metals — generate
carbon credits by displacing the energy-intensive production of virgin
materials. The credit equals the difference in lifecycle CO₂e between
producing the material from raw feedstock versus from recycled
feedstock. Metals, especially aluminum, have the highest credit values
because primary production from ore requires extreme energy.

**1. Master Recyclables Formula**

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 100%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td style="text-align: center;"><p><strong>► Credits (kg CO₂e) = W ×
(EF_virgin − EF_recycled) × QF × VS × LF</strong></p>
<p>Simplified form (pre-computed net EF):</p>
<p><strong>► Credits (kg CO₂e) = W × EF_net × CM × VS × LF</strong></p>
<p>W = Weight submitted (kg)</p>
<p>EF_net = Net emission factor = EF_virgin − EF_recycled (kg
CO₂e/kg)</p>
<p>CM = Credit multiplier (market &amp; purity adjustment)</p>
<p>VS = Verification score (0.50 | 0.85 | 1.00)</p>
<p>LF = Locality factor (0.90–1.10)</p>
<p>QF = Quality/purity factor (0.60–1.00, clean=1.0,
contaminated=0.6)</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

**5. Metals Formulas**

> *Metals — especially aluminum — have the highest credit values of any
> recyclable. Producing aluminum from bauxite ore uses 14× more energy
> than from recycled aluminum. Every kg of aluminum recycled avoids 8.14
> kg CO₂e.*
